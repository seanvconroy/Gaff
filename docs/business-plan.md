# Gaff: business plan

Draft, 31 October 2026. This summarises the current pitch deck. Figures marked "estimate" are ours; prices and targets are hypotheses to test in pilots.

## In one line

Gaff runs every letting for Irish agents and landlords, from first enquiry to signed tenancy. Landlords and agents pay when a home is let; renters never pay.

## The problem

Well-priced homes in Dublin draw hundreds of enquiries each, sometimes over a thousand, and agents cannot answer them. Renters report reply rates from about 1 in 150 to 2 in 10. Fewer than 2,400 homes were advertised to rent nationally in August 2026, so every letting is a scramble: mass viewings, chasing, documents sent to strangers, and silence.

## The root cause

Daft's job ends where the hard part begins. It advertises the home and emails the enquiries on. Everything after that (replying, screening, viewings, choosing, paperwork) is done by hand, by email, phone and Word forms. That is where Gaff works.

## Why now

- **The law:** since 1 March 2026, new tenancies roll into six-year cycles, rent can only reset to market between tenancies in set cases, and setting rent needs three comparables from the RTB Rent Register. Every letting is now a six-year decision that has to be done right.
- **AI:** software can now read and answer every enquiry, sort applicants and draft paperwork at almost no cost.
- **The incumbent is moving:** REA Group paid €248m for 35% of Daft's owner (September 2026) and runs rental application tools in Australia. The window to become agents' letting tool is open now.
- **Landlords are professionalising:** landlords with 100+ tenancies hold 15.6% of private tenancies (9.5% in 2023), and 6,568 landlords hold 6 to 99.

## The product

An AI assistant that runs the letting and asks people for three decisions: who views, who is offered the home, and what is signed. It answers every enquiry, sorts applicants fairly against the operator's criteria, books viewings, checks the chosen applicant (identity, affordability, references), sends the offer, tells everyone else, and prepares the tenancy, payment request and RTB registration. Irish rules are built into the flow. Details are in the PRD.

## The business model

- **About €79 per home let**, paid by the agent or landlord (to test). For comparison, an agent's fee is about one month's rent plus VAT (roughly €2,260 at the average new rent of €1,839) and a private Daft advert costs about €200 to €320.
- **Renters free, always.** Irish law bars charging renters to secure a tenancy, and free is how we win them.
- **Later:** a done-for-you letting service through a licensed partner; referencing and insurance through partners; a monthly plan for landlords with many homes; listing fees once landlords list with us.

## The vision: three steps to owning how Ireland lets homes

1. **The tool (now):** agents and landlords run every letting through Gaff, alongside Daft. We earn a fee per letting. Proof: agents pay and keep using it.
2. **The renters (as we grow):** every applicant gets a free, verified Gaff profile and always hears back. Landlords offer new homes to Gaff renters first. Proof: homes let on Gaff before they reach Daft.
3. **The marketplace (the goal):** landlords list on Gaff, renters search on Gaff, and the letting happens on Gaff. Daft becomes optional. Then homes for sale.

Each step pays for the next and makes Gaff harder to copy.

## Why we can win

Every Irish challenger portal we found since 2005 closed, was bought by Daft's owner, or stayed small. They fought Daft for listings first. We do it in the other order: become the tool agents run lettings on, earn the renters through it, and only then list homes. OpenTable (sold for $2.6bn in 2014) won restaurants with a booking tool before it was a marketplace. In Sweden, Booli overtook Hemnet's listings (89,000 to 55,000 by September 2026), and Hemnet made basic listings free.

Our moat: depth in Irish rules, being each agent's record of every letting, outcome data from every letting, and distribution through agents' own software.

## Market

- About 46,000 homes let in Ireland each year (estimate; 40,000 to 55,000).
- The tool's whole Irish market is about €3m to €4m a year (estimate, working below). That is the ceiling, not our revenue: the tool is our way in, not the prize.
- The prize is the marketplace. REA's deal values Daft's owner at about €709m (all its brands).
- England has about 820,000 private lets a year (estimate), the natural next market.

## Competition

| Who | Built for | Why it isn't Gaff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daft.ie | Anyone advertising a home | Stops at the enquiry; may add tools with REA |
| Lette | Large build-to-rent operators | Enterprise AI leasing, $6.5m raised (October 2026), heading for the UK |
| Agents' software (Acquaint, Reapit and others) | Letting agents | Records and portal feeds; not built for screening or the new rules. We plug in |
| By hand | Most agents today | Slow, inconsistent, often non-compliant |

Smaller tools exist (for example Properly), but none runs the whole letting for independent agents and portfolio landlords.

## Go-to-market

1. Ten pilots from our own network: independent agents and portfolio landlords, plus one large agency as a design partner.
2. Charge from day one; measure hours saved and days to let on every pilot letting.
3. Grow by referral and through the software agents already use.
4. Renters arrive with every letting, so the renter side grows without ad spend.

## The next 90 days: prove it or stop (proposed)

- **Do:** talk to 20 agents and landlords; run 3 to 5 paid pilots on 50+ real lettings; measure hours per letting and days to let; agree a link-up with one agency software provider.
- **Keep going if:** at least 3 hours saved per letting, homes let at least 30% faster, and most pilots pay at least €50 per letting.
- **Stop or rethink if:** agents won't pay €50 per letting, we save less than an hour, or Daft ships the same before we have 20 paying customers.
